Across three complete cohorts, plain rewriting lowered GPTZero's displayed AI confidence in **0 of 15 answers**. Across the two cohorts that also compared each fixed answer with one relevant asker quotation, confidence decreased in **5 of 10 comparisons**. These are observed counts from small convenience samples, not a validated success rate. Every unchanged result remains in the [results dataset](../studies/results.json).

Evidence-first Writing packages this experimental workflow as `discussion-answer-polish`. The cases below document what happened during development on September 5–6, 2026. They do not establish that the current skill reliably lowers detector scores.

| Complete cohort | Plain revisions with lower AI confidence | Fixed-body quotation comparisons with lower AI confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| September 5 plain pilot | 0/5 | Not tested |
| September 5 quotation transfer | 0/5 | 2/5 |
| September 6 quotation transfer | 0/5 | 3/5 |

These cohorts used GPTZero Basic Scan, displayed Model 4.9b, with questions selected before their scores. The transfer writer selected each short quotation for relevance before scanning and did not rotate or lengthen it afterward. Necessary code stayed in the answers. The public input files preserve the effective editor text, including its rendering differences from Markdown; hashes identify those exact artifacts.

In [Qwen Code #1942](https://github.com/QwenLM/qwen-code/discussions/1942#discussioncomment-18308350), the answer explained command permission rules in normal and YOLO approval modes. Both the [baseline](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-2/qwen-1942/baseline.txt) and [plain revision](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-2/qwen-1942/plain.txt) scored 100% AI. The [same revision with the asker's question](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-2/qwen-1942/quoted.txt) scored **1% AI, 99% mixed, 0% human**. Its configuration, rule priority, and limitations remained intact. The reviewed quotation version was published. This is a whole-document classification change: 99% mixed does not mean 99% human, and 1% AI is not a measured fraction of AI-written words.

[ADK #2194](https://github.com/google/adk-python/discussions/2194#discussioncomment-18308383) concerned collecting required arguments across conversation turns. Its [plain answer](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-1/adk-2194/plain.txt) retained the Python example, validation requirements, state reassignment, and treatment of valid zero values. It scored 100% AI. Adding the fixed contextual question produced [40% AI, 60% mixed, 0% human](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-1/adk-2194/quoted.txt), and that version was published. Because the body stayed fixed, this comparison supports an observed quotation effect for this input, not an effect from improving its prose.

The failures matter equally. Poetry #9928, Qwen #1298, Datasets #7131, Poetry #10430, and ADK #1956 all remained at 100% after quotation. In [ADK #1956](https://github.com/google/adk-python/discussions/1956#discussioncomment-18308024), the revision removed an unverified screenshot description while preserving uncertainty about historical API deprecation. The [baseline](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-2/adk-1956/baseline.txt), [plain answer](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-2/adk-1956/plain.txt), and [quotation version](../studies/inputs/quote-transfer-2/adk-1956/quoted.txt) still scored 100%. They displayed 82, 78, and 84 words, with the batch record noting the under-100-word warning. No padding was added. Technical improvement did not produce a detector improvement, and the existing public reply was kept.

Earlier exploratory work shaped these tests but is excluded from their totals. In [ADK #1742](https://github.com/google/adk-python/discussions/1742#discussioncomment-18308381), several prose revisions stayed at 100%. Version 8, which also added a quotation, scored 43% AI. The separate evaluator rejected its implication that recording sent emails prevents duplicate delivery: sending can succeed before recording does. Version 9 changed that advice to checking the sent-email record before resending. It passed review at **60% AI, 40% mixed, 0% human** and was published. The lower-scoring incorrect version was retained as a rejected attempt. This adaptive sequence changed prose and quotation together and intentionally cut some optional operational detail; it is not a controlled quotation comparison.

A subsequent exploratory fixed-body quotation test on [Poetry #10095](https://github.com/python-poetry/poetry/discussions/10095#discussioncomment-18307946) scored 94% AI after its plain revision scored 100%. The reserved [ADK #1513](https://github.com/google/adk-python/discussions/1513#discussioncomment-18308044) check remained at 100%. These helped motivate the two fresh transfer cohorts; neither is included in the 5/10 count.

Four later answers in `mattpocock/skills` had no preexisting owned-answer baseline or unquoted control. [The Wayfinder reply in #231](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/discussions/231#discussioncomment-18313297) scored **93% AI, 7% mixed, 0% human**; [the OpenSpec reply in #285](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/discussions/285#discussioncomment-18313327) scored **11% AI, 89% mixed, 0% human**. Both were published. Their [#231 input](../studies/inputs/new-answers/mattpocock-231/final.txt) and [#285 input](../studies/inputs/new-answers/mattpocock-285/final.txt) each included relevant quoted context. The other new answers, #553 and #287, scored 100% and were withheld. These are unpaired observations, with no before/after improvement claim.

Scores here are maintainer-recorded observations of completed, visibly up-to-date UI scans. There is no authenticated GPTZero export or independent replication. The separate technical and prose reviews were performed by LLM agents without detector scores, not independent human raters. Publication and exact readback were checked; usefulness, acceptance, and human authorship were not measured. Each cohort used a dated development snapshot, and the service or skill can change. The evidence supports retaining failures, checking meaning before publication, and reporting quotation effects separately from prose revisions.
