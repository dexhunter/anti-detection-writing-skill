# Anti-AI Detection Writing Skill

Make AI-written prose easier and more natural to read while preserving its meaning. Improve flow, specificity, and tone for the intended audience. For a short private retailer or customer-service email, answer the latest question first, preserve every supplied fact and qualification, make one clear ask, and use ordinary words. When the user explicitly requests detector testing, work toward lower scores through useful edits and measure the outcome. Never trade accuracy or readability for a score. When they conflict, report both and keep natural, fact-complete English. A fluency polish of a scored draft is a new text, and a paragraph score is not the assembled document's score: rescan the exact final bytes. Detector results do not establish human authorship, inbox delivery, or permission to publish. A lower score is a comparison between comparable scans, not a property of one result or of an unmeasured edit.

## Establish the purpose and facts

1. Save the original text. Identify its reader, purpose, format, and requested tone or length from the supplied context. For a discussion reply, also read the question and existing replies. For a private retailer or customer-service email, follow Private retailer and customer-service email. For public participation, respect the destination's rules, explicit objections to AI-assisted work, and disclosure requirements.
2. Treat supplied facts as the starting point for a self-contained edit. Verify substantive factual corrections against relevant sources; for technical advice, inspect official documentation or implementation and record the version when it matters. Distinguish source inspection, local tests, and deployment verification. Flag unsupported claims instead of adding invented evidence.
3. Inventory the substantive claims, commands, code, conditions, uncertainty, and reported symptoms. Record each claim's time scope: a dated observation and a limitation that still applies are different facts. For each claim record whether it is preserved, corrected, or removed, and why. Identify what the reader needs to understand, decide, or do.

## Private retailer and customer-service email

Use this section for a short private email or message to a retailer, marketplace seller, or customer-service desk. For that email, this section wins every conflict with Write and review. Write and review applies only where this section is silent. Public discussion replies, articles, and documentation stay with Write and review.

Put the facts first. Open with the answer to the latest question, then the other supplied facts and qualifications, then one clear ask. Use ordinary words. Keep every supplied fact and qualification, including limits, dates, conditions, and uncertainty. Concision is wording and order. It does not omit a supplied fact, and the linked-evidence scope cut does not apply to this email. Keep the user's sign-off immediately after the ask, including the closing and name they supplied. When they supplied no sign-off, leave the email without an added name, title, or contact detail.

Work each supplied fact into a sentence that is already there: the answer, the fault, or the ask. Do not add a standalone "Please note..." sentence or a reminder sentence that only lists facts. A bolted-on recap or date sentence, and the sign-off or signature block, are the first places to check when a requested scan flags the draft. On one synthetic kettle email, folding a date into an existing sentence, and avoiding the persona name Helen, removed those two triggers. That is one email. It is not a way to pass a detector. Change how a fact is placed or phrased. Never drop a fact, an uncertainty, or a qualification to lower a score. The sender's own name is a fact: keep the name they supplied, and never replace it with a different name to lower a score.

Later preliminary probes on other synthetic emails (Stages 10–15) found further limits. They are not a way to pass GPTZero. Deleting text from a human-scoring raw draft can flip the score to 100% AI: that happened on the jacket (18% to 100%) and the blender (1% to 100%); the kettle deletion-only control stayed at 4%. On the blender raw, deleting an invented address was the single edit that flipped 2% to 100%, while adding real facts into existing sentences (countertop and date, fault details, a refund line) stayed at 5–10% AI. That address deletion did not flip the jacket raw (29% after deletion, 38% after a fact-neutral replacement). Human-leaning blender scores so far still depended on invented details such as an address or a model number. A fact-complete blender with those invented details removed scored 100% AI, including two later versions where the base model wrote the replacement wording. Never invent an address, a model number, or any other fact to chase a score. Keep every real fact, and never invent one.

**[evidence + our scans]** When a known human-scoring version of this email exists, start from that version and change as little as possible. Do not run a whole-text AI polish or rewrite pass over fact-dense text. On 13 November 2026, free-plan GPTZero Model 5.0b scored one synthetic pot-handle complaint, and 11 fresh rewrites of it, at 100% AI. The rewrites included plain, passive, mixed, timeline, legal, and casual styles, signed and unsigned, and one built from a previous human-scoring email. A rescan of that earlier email stayed at 3% AI. Whole-text rewriting is not a way to pass GPTZero. The table is the [Stage 16 report](../../docs/reports/stage-16-pot-handle.md).

**[our scans; mechanism hypothesis]** Add a new fact inside a sentence that is already there, for example as a relative clause. Do not add a new fact as a bracketed parenthetical, and do not append it as its own sentence. Change one thing, then scan, before changing anything else. On that complaint, the parenthetical "(bought on 18 October 2026, £42.75 paid with Shop Pay)", which added three facts, scored 100% AI. The photos fact folded into the middle of an existing sentence scored 7% AI (177 words). The same fact as a clause on the first sentence scored 100% AI, and as its own final sentence scored 100% AI. One appended photos sentence on the human-scoring control scored 57% AI. These rows are one email. They do not explain the model, and they are not a way to pass GPTZero.

**[our scans]** Small edits on other synthetic complaint emails (15–16 November 2026, Model 5.0b Basic, three free accounts shared across the probes, single scans unless replicated) gave no general lever. Brackets around words already in the email changed nothing (four emails, 0 points), so the bracket rule above is about adding facts, not punctuation; use brackets where they read naturally. Sentence-rhythm and punctuation changes, a paragraph split, and removing stock connectives ("Unfortunately", "As such", "therefore") left 100% emails at 100%. Single-word synonym swaps moved human-scoring emails by up to 8 points, and the direction was not stable. Do not swap synonyms to chase a score. Moving the request to the start dropped two emails (100 to 8, and 100 to 54, each replicated once, at 8 and at 54) but left four other 100% emails at 100%, and a natural rewrite of the 8% email with the request first scored 100%. Keep the request last and the English natural. None of this is a way to pass GPTZero. Details: [detector research](references/detector-research.md#short-email-edit-probes-checked-16-november-2026).

**[evidence]** Never invent a detail, and never delete a fact, an uncertainty, or a qualification, to chase a score. Before any requested scan, run `scripts/check_facts.py` on the fact-complete source and the candidate. If it reports a missing, changed, or added number, date, price, order ID, or capitalised name, correct the candidate before scanning. The checker does not judge style or a detector score.

**[hypothesis]** Prefer plain first person, and keep legal boilerplate minimal, where the facts allow. This is a readability preference. The 13 November 2026 scans do not show that plain first person, or shorter legal wording, lowers a GPTZero score.

Write one email to one recipient. Do not prepare a follow-up or a copy for another recipient unless the user asks for that separate email. The one allowed extra wording is a measured alternative the user explicitly requests: keep the original as the control, preserve every supplied fact and qualification, and do not run a scan. It is not a second recipient.

When the supplied draft already answers the latest question, keeps every supplied fact and qualification, uses one clear ask and ordinary words, and keeps the user's sign-off, leave that draft unchanged and say so. Still apply a requested factual correction or a sign-off the user asked to add. Also fold a bolted-on recap or date sentence into a sentence already there, and keep the fact. That fold removed a date-sentence trigger on one kettle email. It is not a way to pass a detector.

Never invent personal experience, an injury, storage or carrying details, a cause, a date, tests, certainty, identity, quotations, typos, slang, deliberate errors, or invisible characters. Keep an unknown fact unknown. When the email cannot proceed without a missing fact, ask for that fact in the one ask.

Do not copy a public asker quotation, or any other quoted line, into private mail to humanize it or to seek a lower score. Do not add, remove, or alter a fact, uncertainty, or qualification to chase a detector score. Change how it is placed or phrased instead. A seller's claim that the text is AI-written, or a report that spam filtering held it, does not ask for a detector scan. It establishes no baseline, model, score, or reason for filtering. Do not promise a human classification, a lower detector score, or inbox delivery. The kettle sentences above limit that one email. They are not a method that passes a detector. A lower score requires a comparable scan the user explicitly requested.

## Write and review

- Separate the source inventory from the answer inventory. For a direct question, keep the conclusion, necessary support, and qualifications that change the reader's decision; leave other background in the linked evidence. Record scope cuts rather than carrying every source fact into the reply.
- Rebuild the text around the reader's purpose rather than retaining the original sentence order. Put the main point where the format calls for it, connect each sentence to the next, and keep the detail needed to understand or act on it. After moving evidence, check paragraph density: a paragraph that combines the intervention, results, screenshot explanation and limitations may need a break even when every sentence is accurate.
- For a procedure or causal explanation, map each action to its component, prerequisite, and limiting condition. Arrange the answer in that dependency order. Place a prerequisite before the action that needs it and keep an exception beside the claim it limits. Replace an ambiguous pronoun with the existing component name. Keep unspecified actors unnamed, including in intermediate fact tables: “All 180 articles have been copied” does not establish that a migration team copied them. Preserve passive voice when naming an actor would add an unsupported fact. For ordinary polishing, leave already clear passages intact. When the user explicitly requests a measured alternative, retain the original as a control and allow a different reader-appropriate framing that preserves every supplied fact, qualification, and the practical meaning.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Remove generic reassurance, repeated summaries, rhetorical contrasts, and irrelevant background. Use lists only where they make steps or comparisons easier to follow. After factual repair, check for stiff noun phrases and overloaded steps; moving unchanged sentences may leave those problems intact.
- For vocabulary cleanup, use the [phrase review list](references/phrase-review.md). Avoid stock phrases when they add no meaning; review matches in context rather than banning words or swapping synonyms mechanically. Preserve precise terminology, intended tone, uncertainty and protected spans. An absent phrase needs no edit, and a cleaner vocabulary is not evidence of a lower detector score. **[evidence]** A synonym swap, an "AI word" list, invisible characters, DIPPER, and a commercial humaniser are not detector levers. Use a plainer word when it is easier to read. Do not swap synonyms, strip a listed word, insert an invisible character, run DIPPER, or run a commercial humaniser to seek a lower score. The [13 November 2026 literature update](../../docs/literature-update-2026-11-13.md) records why that does not transfer to GPTZero Model 5.0b. A later back-translation experiment does not reopen those levers. The [16 November 2026 literature update](../../docs/literature-update-2026-11-16.md) restates that limit.
- **[evidence]** During a requested detector iteration, rescan the exact final assembled document. A paragraph score is not that document's score. Treat a fluency polish of a low-scoring draft as a new text and rescan those bytes. When readability and a lower score conflict, report both and keep natural, fact-complete English. Round-trip translation is an experiment only when the user has asked for detector iteration, not a default rewrite. Repair only factual drift and genuine grammar errors, fact-check, and warn that odd phrasing may be doing the work. Do not assume a pivot language. Fixing the worst phrases one at a time and rescanning is one observed sequence on a single letter. It is not a bypass and not a recipe. The measured case is one private recommendation letter on 16 November 2026, free-plan GPTZero Model 5.0b: c4 100% AI, c7 15%, c7a 13%, c7b 43%, c8 100%. The user chose the natural c8. The limits are in [detector research](references/detector-research.md). This is one letter, one account, and a single scan of each version. It is not a way to pass GPTZero.
- Preserve operational qualifications and exact code unless evidence supports a correction. Record deliberate scope cuts; do not call an answer fully claim-preserving when useful details were removed.
- Polish the explanatory message independently of its code. Keep code blocks and standalone commands fixed during prose experiments; assess their correctness separately, and record any necessary technical correction. Keep inline identifiers and values where they are part of an explanation. The full message, including its code, still needs a consistency review.
- Match the requested register. If an authentic writing sample is supplied, identify its register, paragraph openings, and transitions before drafting; preserve useful habits during cleanup without importing the sample's facts, opinions, or personal history. Vary sentence length when it improves pacing; do not force informality or strip useful structure. Never invent personal experience, injuries, storage or carrying details, causes, dates, tests, certainty, identity, quotations, typos, slang, deliberate errors, or invisible characters to make text appear human-written.
- A short, exact asker quotation can identify the concern being answered in a public discussion reply. Include it only when it helps that reader. Verify context and attribution; do not pad or rotate quotations to hunt for a detector score. Do not import that technique into private mail to humanize it.
- Compare the finished answer directly with the claim inventory. Check who acts, negation, cause and effect, scope, certainty, numbers, units, and prerequisites. Keep an ongoing limitation current; changing “has not been validated” to “had not been validated at that point” narrows the claim unless the evidence supports that change. Match protected code, commands, identifiers, URLs, and exact quotations byte-for-byte unless a verified correction is recorded. Account for every cut or correction and remove unsupported additions. This writer self-check does not replace separate review.
- When a separate reviewer is requested or available, give them the reader's purpose, relevant context and sources, baseline, final text, and claim inventory without detector scores. Request a verdict on correctness, relevance, readability, tone, and necessary qualifications, tied to the final file's SHA-256. Resolve factual objections even if an incorrect version has a lower score. If no separate reviewer is available, say so; a self-check is not independent review.
- **[our scans; hedged]** When a human-written draft already exists, polish it lightly and keep similarity to that draft at roughly 0.75 or higher. That figure is a conservative margin on this sample, not a tested cutoff. Similarity is 1 − Levenshtein/max_len: Levenshtein is the character edit distance, and max_len is the length of the longer text. This repo does not include a script for that ratio. Check facts against the draft. On free-plan GPTZero Model 5.0b Basic scans of public Enron AESLC test-split emails (Stages 28–31), LLM edits of human drafts at similarity of at least 0.70 stayed classed human in 8/8 recorded edits (a1, a2, a3, b1, b2, f1, g1, h1). That 8/8 is eight edits, not eight drafts. Seven of those eight were 0–6% AI. g1 rose to 21% (+19), past the about 2-point rescan band, on the assessment/judgement-style draft; its similarity was 0.751. This only preserved a human classification when the starting draft was already human-written. It does not lower the score of text that starts AI-written, and it does not guarantee 0%. Keeping the draft's casual voice was not supported (a4, similarity 0.502, scored 100% AI). The committed rows name 7 drafts (AESLC test rows 50, 57, 65, 79, 101, 119, and 170). An eighth draft is not in those rows and is not counted. Lengths were not logged. There was one upward drift (g1), and it was not tested on long documents. This note does not replace the rules above, and it is not a way to pass GPTZero. Details: [detector research](references/detector-research.md#stages-26-31-checked-16-17-november-2026) and the [GPTZero results report](../../docs/reports/gptzero-results.md).
- **[our scans; hedged]** When a heavy edit of a human-written draft is actually needed, one small-sample observation is to keep at least one of that draft's own sentences word for word, and keep similarity to the draft at about 0.70 or higher where the edit still allows it. That pairing is a conservative margin on this sample, not a tested cutoff. On free-plan GPTZero Model 5.0b scans of three short public Enron AESLC test-split emails, scores read in Basic view, restoring any one sentence of the human draft verbatim inside a heavy LLM rewrite brought those three risen edits back to a human label on this sample (Stage 33: 6/6 reverts on rows 50, 57, and 79; drops of 86–91, 22–24, and 14 points), past the about 2-point rescan band. Stage 34 rescanned the unchanged bases in pure Basic mode at 100, 24, and 14, matching the earlier scores, so those drops are not explained by scan mode. Which sentence was restored made little difference (open-versus-control gaps −5, −2, and 0, within or near noise), so an opening-line-specific hypothesis is not supported. Stage 32 is one text only (row 119, g) and stays a hunch: the g0 rescan was 2% AI and the g1 rescan was 21% AI. The 0.751 similarity is the Stage 31 figure for g1, not a new measurement. Four single-sentence restorations scored 0%, 1%, 14%, and 35% AI. The 35% score is above the 21% g1 rescan, so that stage does not show that every restoration lowered the score. Each of the six Stage 33 reverts also raised character similarity by about 0.07–0.14 (similarities 0.600, 0.601, 0.616, 0.609, 0.674, and 0.672, all below 0.70), so keeping a verbatim sentence and staying closer to the draft are not yet separated. The 0.70 figure is a margin carried from Stages 28–31, not a cutoff these six reverts tested. Only three short business emails. This applies only when the starting draft is genuinely human-written. It does not lower the score of text that starts AI-written, and fresh or polished natural rewrites still score 100% AI. It does not guarantee 0%. GPTZero Model 5.0b only. Preserve every fact, uncertainty, and qualification. Never add errors or typos. This note does not replace the light-edit note above, and it is not a way to pass GPTZero. Details: [detector research](references/detector-research.md#stages-32-34) and the [GPTZero results report](../../docs/reports/gptzero-results.md#stages-32-34).

## Measure only when requested

Read [measurement instructions](references/measurement.md) before live scanning and [evidence limits](references/evidence.md) before interpreting results. Use the user's requested service, mode, and supported browser tools. This skill supplies no browser controls and does not authorize subscriptions, paid scans, public posting, or new account access.

Run a scan only when the user explicitly asks for a detector measurement. A seller's claim that a message is AI-written, or a report that spam filtering held it, is not that request and does not establish a baseline, a displayed model, a score, or why a filter acted. Do not promise that any revision will be classified as human-written or delivered to an inbox.

When explaining detector mechanisms or planning another batch after unchanged results, read [detector research](references/detector-research.md).

When the user explicitly asks for an autoresearch workflow to improve this skill, read the [bounded research workflow](references/autoresearch.md). It separates skill proposals, preference-based selection, quality review and measured confirmation; ordinary writing does not need this workflow.

For a comparison, select cases and candidates before seeing scores. Record fresh baseline and candidate scans using the same service, mode, and displayed model. Retain failures and unchanged results. Compare a fixed body with a relevant quotation separately from prose rewriting; track content and length changes. Distinguish first-pass trials from adaptive iterations. Record an unchanged candidate as a no-op. Comparing it with a separately rewritten baseline measures preservation versus that rewrite, not a benefit from edits the candidate did not make.

When a scan runs, the detector target is the message's prose unless the user explicitly requests the whole message. Declare `prose_only` before testing, exclude only independently identified code blocks or standalone command lines, and retain all explanatory prose, qualifications and inline identifiers. Save the full reviewed message, exact prose input and code-range mapping separately. Use the same scope for both arms; keep earlier whole-message scores under their original scope. An explicit whole-message request uses `full_text` instead.

Confirm the effective editor text, completed scan, fresh-result indicator, model, native result labels and metrics, timestamp, and warnings. Save exact inputs and private visible evidence. A stale displayed number is not a result for changed text. Never pad short prose, add code back merely to reach a detector minimum, or drop a fact, an uncertainty, a qualification, or a difficult explanatory passage to lower a score. Change how a fact is placed or phrased instead.

When a requested scan flags a short email, check a bolted-on recap or date sentence and the sign-off or signature block before rewriting the rest. To locate a trigger sentence, follow one-fact restoration in the [measurement instructions](references/measurement.md): scan a deletions-only control, then variants that each add one fact back. Three other synthetic emails built the same way, with plain sender names, each scored 100% AI. Date folding and avoiding a stock persona name removed triggers on the kettle email. They are not a way to pass GPTZero. The control is missing facts, so it is not the email to send, and a real sender's name stays as supplied. ## Publish and report

Honor existing user authorization; do not invent authorization or ask again for an already authorized action. When the text is a private retailer or customer-service email, return that one email to the user, do not post it, and do not send it unless the user has explicitly authorized the send. For a public reply, refresh the discussion before posting and reassess if context changed. Match the full final body to the reviewed version and the declared detector input to its scan and requested threshold. A prose-only result describes the prose, not the complete message or its code. Under `prose_only`, changing only code requires renewed full-message review and an updated prose mapping; if the measured prose bytes stay identical, its receipt still describes those bytes. Under `full_text`, a code change also requires a new scan. Update the owned answer when editing, then independently read back its author, exact body, and URL. Do not post a duplicate or claim an answer was accepted without checking.

Report the actual quality findings, scores and warnings when measured, and edit or keep decisions. When a natural, fact-complete wording and a lower detector score conflict, report both. The text to use is the natural wording. Keep the low-scoring draft as a scored backup. On 16 November 2026, for one private recommendation letter, the user chose the natural version (c8, 100% AI) over the awkward draft (c7, 15% AI). Missing required review or measurement leaves a candidate unpublished. A detector threshold applies only if requested. A successful local receipt check never authorizes publication.

For a showcase, include only quality-passing candidates with a measured decrease against a saved before-text using comparable completed scans. Link exact inputs, identify the intervention and tested version, and keep the full comparison denominator visible. Put unchanged, worse, rejected, unpaired, and unmeasured outcomes in a separate limitations section while retaining all records. A quotation effect is not a rewriting effect, and an untested skill revision is not an established improvement. If a case does not improve, document when the approach may not help or test a specific new edit within a bounded experiment.
