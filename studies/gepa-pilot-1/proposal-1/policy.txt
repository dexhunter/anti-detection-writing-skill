## Write and review

- Start with the reader's purpose and the claim inventory. Distinguish required answer claims from optional source background. Keep every claim the task requires, including its uncertainty and conditions. Record any permitted scope cut; do not call a shortened answer fully claim-preserving when it removes useful detail.
- For light copyediting, retain passages that already work. When asked to produce or rewrite a polished answer from supplied material, save the baseline, set its wording aside, and rebuild sentence boundaries from the inventory. Do not begin by moving or replacing the baseline's sentences. First group claims that describe the same action, condition, or consequence. Give each required claim a home in one group; preserve a second occurrence only when the reader needs the reminder.
- Draft those groups as connected prose, using a concrete action or result as each group's grammatical center. Integrate an abstract statement of purpose into the action that demonstrates it when that sentence can express both claims accurately. Keep a separate definition or opening claim when the reader needs it to follow the mechanism. This is a clause-allocation edit: change which claims share a sentence and which claim carries its main verb, rather than merely changing the order or format. Do not force every group into one sentence.
- Review each proposed sentence boundary for its effect on comprehension. Join dependent fragments of an explanation when the second only completes the first; split when another actor, independent condition, or qualification makes the sentence hard to follow. Keep a condition attached to exactly the result it governs. A shorter sentence must not turn a conditional statement into an unconditional promise. Do not merge distinct cases merely because their language resembles each other.
- Read each paragraph as a sequence of information handoffs. Let the next sentence begin from an object, action, or consequence the reader can already identify, then add the next necessary fact. Use a component's name when a pronoun would be ambiguous. Follow the actual dependencies: prerequisites precede the actions that need them, and exceptions stay beside the claims they limit. Do not impose identical paragraph shapes, a fixed sentence-length pattern, a question-and-answer format, or ornamental transitions.
- Keep the answer direct and suited to the requested register. Put the main answer early, using the concrete explanation itself when it can answer the question without a separate preview. End when the last necessary fact or qualification is complete. Remove redundant previews, recaps, generic reassurance, and irrelevant background. Use a list when the reader needs separately addressable steps or comparisons, not to obtain a different appearance.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Use the [phrase review list](references/phrase-review.md) only to diagnose a real reading problem. Review matches in context; do not impose word bans, swap synonyms mechanically, or rewrite an absent phrase. Preserve precise terminology, tone, and protected spans. Cleaner vocabulary and different syntax are not evidence of a lower detector score.
- Preserve the inventory's actors, temporal scope, cause and effect, and degree of certainty. Leave an unspecified actor unnamed; use passive voice when naming an actor would add a fact. Keep ongoing limitations current. Preserve operational qualifications even when they complicate a sentence. Never invent experience, tests, identity, guarantees, quotations, deliberate mistakes, invisible characters, or irrelevant padding. Do not add or rotate quotations for measurement. Retain an existing verified quotation only when it serves the reader.
- If an authentic writing sample is supplied, use its register and useful habits without importing its facts, opinions, or personal history. Sentence variety should follow the content and improve pacing; do not manufacture informality, asymmetry, or choppiness.
- Edit explanatory prose independently of code. Keep code blocks and standalone commands fixed during a prose experiment; have their correctness assessed separately and record any necessary technical correction. Keep inline identifiers and values in the explanation. Review the complete message for agreement between prose and code.
- Compare the finished answer directly with the claim inventory and sources. Check actors, negation, prerequisites, conditional branches, scope, temporal meaning, certainty, numbers, and units. Check the new sentence groupings especially for conditions that now appear to govern an extra claim or no longer govern their original claim. Match protected code, commands, identifiers, URLs, and quotations byte-for-byte unless a verified correction is recorded. Account for every cut and correction; remove unsupported additions. Revert an edit that reduces accuracy or readability.
- A writer's self-check does not replace independent review. When a separate reviewer is requested or available, provide the reader's purpose, context and sources, baseline, exact final text, and claim inventory without detector scores. Ask for a verdict on correctness, relevance, readability, tone, necessary qualifications, and code consistency, tied to the final file's SHA-256. Resolve factual objections regardless of measurement results. If independent review is unavailable, report that limitation accurately.
