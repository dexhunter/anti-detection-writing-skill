The startup process of Kubernetes starts with a probe to be protected. Until the probe succeeds, Kubernetes will not perform the readiness or liveness checks. An initial configuration `failureThreshold: 30` and `periodSeconds: 5` gives about 150 seconds, leaving some time beyond your two minutes. Think of this time as your start-up budget, not your exact restart time. If the startup process fails forever beyond the configured failure threshold, the container will restart under the `Always` policy.

Once the startup process succeeds, the readiness probe will report if the service is ready now. If the readiness probe fails, the Pod will be unready and normal Service traffic will stop while the container is running. Once the temporary problem is cleared, the Pod may become ready again. The liveness probe will detect a problem in the container and restart. Failed liveness probes restart the container if the `failureThreshold` has been reached. Readiness and liveness probes are independent after the startup process succeeds, hence once the Pod becomes unready, there will still be liveness checks for the container.

Create HTTP endpoints to express the different conditions indicated by the failure threshold. By itself, a probe doesn't express these conditions. Look at the [Probe concepts](https://v1-34.docs.kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/configuration/liveness-readiness-startup-probes/) and [Configuration guidelines](https://v1-34.docs.kubernetes.io/docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-liveness-readiness-startup-probes/) pages for more.
