Juniper Reach Library should extend its room-booking pilot for six weeks to test access and reliability before considering more rooms. The first eight weeks show demand, without establishing reliable access.

Two rooms offered free, two-hour bookings during staffed hours: 118 of 160 slots were reserved, and 91 were used. Of 42 voluntary feedback respondents, 25 valued predictable space for study or small-group work; 17 struggled to find a slot or complete a booking. These responses cannot establish how widespread those difficulties are.

Eighteen of 27 unused reservations belonged to four repeat bookers. Most slots never reserved fell before noon on weekdays. Cancellation habits and residents’ availability may explain these patterns; neither explanation is established. Unused reservations alone do not measure unmet demand, and adding rooms would not necessarily address either problem.

The online form requires an email address, while telephone bookings depend on the front-desk supervisor’s availability. Three of the 17 respondents reporting difficulties said the email requirement or limited telephone coverage prevented a booking. During the extension, any trained desk worker should accept telephone and in-person reservations, with every route using the same inventory. Reminders and simple cancellation could help release unwanted reservations, though effectiveness remains unmeasured. Releasing a slot does not establish that someone uses it.

The service manager should arrange training and publish weekly reservation, attendance, cancellation and unaccommodated-request totals by booking route and time of day. Desk workers should record requests consistently, including when rooms are full.

After six weeks, the proposed conditions for considering expansion are trained staff able to cover every staffed opening period, at least 85% of reservations used or cancelled before their start, and unaccommodated requests persisting in at least four of those weeks despite the changes. Meeting all three would support considering expansion, because continuing requests would be assessed alongside staff coverage and booking reliability. The conditions would not establish how many rooms are needed. If coverage or reliability remains weak, revise the process and reassess before expanding.
