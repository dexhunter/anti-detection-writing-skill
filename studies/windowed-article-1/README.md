# Five-window article rewrite

**The fixed control was slightly preferred, so the experiment stopped before detector scanning.** On September 9, 2026, five fresh writers rewrote one development article in sequence. The assembled candidate A had 526 words; the previously fixed control B had 538. One independent masked reviewer passed both on all 18 listed claims, all seven protected literals, and suitability. The frozen gate required a candidate preference or tie. There were zero scans, no repair, no article publication, no method promotion and no confirmation run. See the [outcome](outcome.json) and [final review](artifacts/quality-review/review.md).

Each writer received one contiguous source window, its claims and literals, a common operator, and the immutable text already accepted from earlier windows. There was one output per window and no whole-article cleanup. The exact [source](artifacts/source.txt), [candidate A](artifacts/candidate.txt), [control B](artifacts/control.txt), [operator](artifacts/operator.txt), [frozen protocol](artifacts/protocol.json) and [assembly](artifacts/assembly.json) are retained. Each `artifacts/window-1/` through `artifacts/window-5/` directory contains the exact source, packet, prefix, output, writer self-check and provisional acceptance record.

The reviewer preferred B's explicit development dates and clearer measurement instructions. A was shorter and made the caption's historical-score qualification more explicit. **The date comparison has a preparation limitation:** the [full case's source metadata](artifacts/source-case.json) specified September 5–6, but the [window 4 packet](artifacts/window-4/packet.json) omitted those metadata and required only a “dated development sample.” A retained that wording; B named the precise range. Both passed because AX13 did not require that range. The preference therefore cannot be attributed entirely to windowing. The control was also previously fixed; model/settings, call count, batching and evidence availability were not experimentally isolated. This was not an isolated prefix ablation or a matched fresh generation pair.

This is a **different-model paragraph-window adaptation of DIPPER**, not a reproduction or a detector result. The [saved research note](artifacts/research/next-methods-scout.md) documents DIPPER's trained paraphraser and default three-sentence prefix loop; this trial used five paragraph windows with different writers. [GPTZero's 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13042v1) includes DIPPER outputs in its training augmentation. Historical DIPPER results therefore provide no assurance of a current detector reduction. Historical scores retained inside the article describe earlier Qwen evidence, not scores for these drafts.

The [method](method.json) records the design and limits. The [manifest](manifest.json) gives every exported file's hash and available original source hashes. Text artifacts retain their exact bytes. Only absolute local paths, task identity and account verification metadata were sanitized; original hashes embedded in historical records remain unchanged and may differ from sanitized copies. The protocol’s original and removed-field digests are retained privately because publishing them would allow recovery of removed account metadata. This closed, unmeasured rejection supports no default writing rule. No scripts, private UI evidence, secrets, sealed data or model weights are included.
