Use a startup probe to protect the initialization period. Until it succeeds, Kubernetes holds off both readiness and liveness checks. An initial configuration of `failureThreshold: 30` and `periodSeconds: 5` provides about 150 seconds, leaving some margin beyond your two-minute startup. Treat this as a starting budget, not an exact restart deadline. If startup keeps failing through that threshold, the container restarts under your `Always` policy.

Once startup succeeds, readiness should answer whether the service can handle requests now. A failed readiness check makes the Pod unready and stops normal Service traffic while the container keeps running. It can become ready again when the temporary problem clears.

Liveness should detect the deadlock that needs a restart. Consecutive failures reaching its configured threshold restart the container. Readiness and liveness are independent after startup, so becoming unready does not suspend liveness checks.

Implement HTTP endpoints that reflect these different conditions; naming a probe alone does not give it that knowledge. See the [probe concepts](https://v1-34.docs.kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/configuration/liveness-readiness-startup-probes/) and [configuration guidance](https://v1-34.docs.kubernetes.io/docs/tasks/configure-pod-container/configure-liveness-readiness-startup-probes/).
